The art of prompting

Before

"Create a website for my course."

Nothing says who the site is for or how it should look, so the AI has to guess.

After · the six parts

  • RoleAn expert educational web designer
  • GoalA responsive site for a 15-lesson course
  • AudienceStudents with little coding experience
  • PagesLanding, curriculum, lessons, about, resources
  • StyleLight, playful, bold headings
  • OutputFolder structure, code, design notes

The speakers' tip. Find "Handoff to Claude Code…" before you start building. It is easy to miss in the menu.

part three

What a site can't do

Two limits of a plain website, and how a backend such as Supabase fills the gap.

I

It forgets everyone

Close the tab and a plain site has no idea who you were. It cannot keep users or saved games.

II

It knows only its files

It cannot show anything live, like the weather. It only shows what was typed into the code.

How logging in works

Sign up

A new row goes into a user table, with your email and a scrambled password.

Log in

The site checks your details. If they match, your browser gets a token.

Log out

The token is thrown away, and the site stops recognising you.

A token is a temporary sticker that says "this is me", so you don't retype your password on every click. It expires on its own. Logging in is just an API call. Supabase provides logins, a user database and usage statistics, and connects to GitHub, Vercel and Lovable.

my reflection

What stayed with me

The biggest lesson was about prompting. "Make a website for my course" gets something generic. A prompt with a role, a goal, an audience, the pages I need and a design style gets something I can use. The quality of what the AI builds depends on how clearly I describe what I want.

I also understood how the pieces connect. GitHub stores the code, but nothing there is a website yet. Vercel turns the repository into a live link and rebuilds it every time I push. Supabase covers what a plain site cannot do: remember users and save data.

Building Fashion Annotations in Lovable showed me that an idea can become a working prototype inside one class. Lovable handled the GitHub connection, so I could spend my time on the tone and the visual system.

The AI writes the code. The brief is still mine to write.
